2. How it was measured

  • Collection: 1748 publications from 5 outlets, article text extracted and reduced to a stored excerpt; related-article teasers, navigation and markup are removed.
  • Name matching: a surname dictionary with Georgian case endings. A bare surname is not enough for shared names: Bidzina Ivanishvili needs his first name or a known alias, and Zurabashvili is never matched to Zourabichvili.
  • Stance: 295 labels from a language model (prompt stance-v5) with four categories — supportive, critical, neutral, unclear — each carrying a verbatim quote from the text.
  • Human check: four rounds of hand labelling by a reviewer who did not see the model's answer first: 37/40, 27/30, 28/30 exact, and 20/20 on a random draw from the full corpus. Critical recall 4 of 5.

3. What this cannot support

  • Not airtime. Website publications are a different corpus from broadcast transcripts and are never merged with it.
  • Supportive is thin. Six rows in the whole corpus. It is reported as a count, never as a rate.
  • Two outlets carry the finding. The comparison rests on Imedi and TV Pirveli, which hold 292 of the 295 stance rows. The other outlets contribute a handful between them, and Rustavi 2 has no editorial leaning recorded at all — it is marked unassigned and excluded from every leaning comparison.
  • This is a classifier, not a reader. Stance labels come from a model. The direction is well supported by the human checks; a single percentage point is not.
